# Venture Architect Committee Charter

The committee exists to improve decisions, not to create more analysis.

## Roles

### 1. Bootstrap Founder
Question: **Can we execute this with cash discipline and founder intensity?**
Owns constraints, founder energy and practical feasibility.

### 2. Customer Advocate
Question: **What customer behavior proves the problem is painful?**
Rejects internal enthusiasm as evidence.

### 3. Growth Operator
Question: **How do we reach the next 1, 10 and 100 customers?**
Owns channel mechanics and conversion bottlenecks.

### 4. CFO
Question: **What happens to cash, margin and payback if this works — and if it does not?**
Owns pricing, cost ceilings and capital allocation.

### 5. Product Architect
Question: **What is the smallest experience that changes customer behavior?**
Owns scope discipline.

### 6. AI / Automation Architect
Question: **Which repeated manual step is now stable enough to automate?**
Automation follows evidence; it does not precede it.

### 7. Investor
Question: **If this works, can it compound into a larger and defensible asset?**
Owns option value and moat path.

### 8. Devil's Advocate
Question: **What would make our story false?**
Must identify at least one disconfirming test.

### 9. Decision Scientist
Question: **What evidence should change the decision?**
Defines measurement, counterfactuals and kill criteria.

### 10. Investment Committee Chair
Question: **Given the evidence, what do we do now?**
The Chair does not average opinions. The Chair decides: continue, modify, stop, defer or escalate.

## Required committee outputs

Every meaningful review ends with exactly:

1. **Decision** — what changes now.
2. **Owner** — one accountable owner.
3. **Expected result** — observable result.
4. **Deadline** — when evidence is due.
5. **Kill criterion** — evidence that stops/reverses the action.
6. **Stop-doing item** — work removed from the system.
7. **Compounding asset** — what remains useful even if the experiment fails.

## Meeting rules

- Facts first; narratives second.
- Customer actions outrank customer compliments.
- Leading indicators do not replace revenue, but revenue is too slow to manage day-to-day.
- A red metric requires diagnosis, not punishment.
- A metric without a decision attached is decoration.
- No more than three company-level priorities at a time.
- Every recurring meeting must earn its continuation.

## Escalation thresholds

**RED**
- zero external conversations after two workdays,
- zero positive replies after 40 high-quality targeted contacts,
- selling time < build time for two consecutive days without a live customer blocker,
- or a critical experiment has no falsifiable success/failure definition.

**AMBER**
- conversations exist but no next step,
- interest without urgency,
- or pipeline depends on one prospect.

**GREEN**
- qualified conversations are increasing,
- repeated pain is converging,
- buyers accept the pilot logic,
- meetings/proposals advance.
